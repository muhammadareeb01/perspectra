By the numbers

News avoidance, 2017 vs 2026

More people are switching the feed off

When Reuters asked people if they sometimes or often dodge the news, 29% said yes in 2017. In 2026 it was 42%. Not everyone hates journalism; plenty are just tired of the noise.

+13pp from 2017 to 2026

Where people get news

News routes · Reuters Institute · 2024

Most people start online news away from publisher homepages

Across 47 markets, the Reuters Institute tracks where respondents say they usually begin online news. Search and aggregators lead, social media is close behind, and direct visits to news sites or apps trail both.

Why we link out. If the feed is already where people land, comparison has to travel with them. Perspectra clusters an event and sends you to the publisher, not a copy on our servers.

How fast falsehoods move

Misinformation research · MIT · 2018

On Twitter, false stories spread faster than the truth

Soroush Vosoughi, Deb Roy, and Sinan Aral studied about 126,000 true and false news cascades on Twitter from 2006 to 2017, checked against independent fact checkers. Humans, not bots, drove most of the gap.

+70%Falsehoods were more likely to be retweeted than the truth in that dataset.

  • ~126ktrue and false cascades studied
  • ~3mpeople in the diffusion maps

Why compare before you share. Reach is not proof. Perspectra lines up who reported what, adds lean and factuality labels, and keeps fact-check culture one click away so you can read originals before amplifying a take.
